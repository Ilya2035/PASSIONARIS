# Industrial minerals, building materials, gems: reference numbers for Passionaris

Research date: 2026-11-11. All units are metric: t, kt = 10^3 t, Mt = 10^6 t, Gt = 10^9 t, Tt = 10^12 t.
Gems are in kg (1 carat = 0.2 g, so 1 Mct = 200 kg). USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries "tons" are metric tons.
Coordinates are approximate (±0.1–0.3°). "My calc" marks numbers I derived; everything else is quoted from the source.

Tonnage types differ by row. Each cell says which one it is: in-place (geological), reserves (economic, proven + probable),
resources (all categories), or produced + remaining.

## Densities and assumptions used in my calculations

- Rock salt 2.16 t/m³.
- Gypsum 2.3 t/m³.
- Limestone 2.6–2.7 t/m³; chalk 1.9–2.2 t/m³.
- Granite 2.65 t/m³; sandstone 2.3–2.5 t/m³.
- Sand and gravel in-bank 1.9 t/m³; dry sand 1.6 t/m³.
- Clay in situ 1.9 t/m³.
- Caliche 2.1 t/m³ (from SQM).
- Obsidian 2.35 t/m³.
- Amber ≈1.05 t/m³.

"Usable share" is the part of suitable ground that is actually quarried, after land use, water table, overburden, quality and
access. It is the most uncertain factor, so per-100-km² values are order-of-magnitude.

Default producers are ≈15–30 years of a mean US operation's output (USGS MCS 2026 production ÷ number of operations).
Pre-industrial producers were 10–100 times smaller. For a historical-epoch game, scale the default down rather than the
deposit.

## Caveats

1. **Size classes are my judgement**, anchored to the cited numbers. Only two come directly from USGS grade–tonnage models:
   - phosphate (Bulletin 1693, Appendix B: upwelling type, log10 tonnage mean 8.52, SD 0.86, n = 60; warm-current type
     8.60 / 0.74 / 18);
   - kimberlite diamonds (median 26 Mt at 0.25 ct/t).

   USGS OFR 92-437 (Orris & Bliss 1992) also has grade–tonnage models for potash-bearing bedded salt, marine and lacustrine
   gypsum, lacustrine borates, fluorite veins, flake and amorphous graphite, silica sand and sandstone silica, and fumarolic
   sulfur. Their percentiles sit only in scanned figures that I could not extract as text. Read them by eye if exact class
   boundaries matter.
2. **The Bulletin 1693 phosphate statistics come from garbled OCR**: I matched columns by position. The medians (≈330 and
   ≈400 Mt) fit the deposits listed, but recheck them against figs. 178–179.
3. **Tonnage types are not interchangeable.**
   - Upper Kama, Green River and White Sands are in-place geological totals: very large and mostly unmineable.
   - Oulad Abdoun, Nueva Victoria and Kırka are economic reserves.
   - Argyle, Grime's Graves and Yogo are produced (+ remaining).

   For gameplay, a reasonable conversion is in-place × 0.1–0.3 ≈ extractable.
4. **"Moderate" choices.**
   - Khewra: 300 Mt (not the 6.68 Gt outlier).
   - Mishraq: ≈250 Mt (official 354–400 Mt).
   - Kagem: attributable 1,088 Mct (not SRK's 1.8 Gct of 2015).
   - Argyle: 865 Mct (not the 900 Mct AusIMM figure).
   - Yogo's "28 Mct remaining" is unsourced.
   - Lapis's 1,300 t comes from a non-authoritative compilation.
5. **Gem carats are mostly not gems.**
   - Kagem and Montepuez carats include low-grade beryl and corundum.
   - Argyle was mostly brown and industrial diamond.

   Gem quality is roughly 1–20% of the weight. Apply a quality multiplier if the game separates gem and industrial grades.
6. **Footprints marked n/a or "estimate" were not found in a source.**
   - Kırka, Balama, Cape Flattery and Hubei: estimated from typical pit sizes or not given.
   - Primorskoye and Lake MacDonnell: back-calculated from tonnage ÷ (thickness × density).
7. **Per-area richness spans eight orders of magnitude**, so the game cannot use one density per material:
   - evaporite salt ≈100–600 Mt/km²;
   - trona ≈33 Mt/km²;
   - phosphate ≈6 Mt/km²;
   - nitrate ≈0.1 Mt/km²;
   - diamonds ≈0.3 t/km² even in the richest pipe.
8. **Not re-verified here:** the 10 Gt figure for Uyuni (tourism and Guinness sources, no primary survey found) and
   Mexico's San Marcos Island gypsum (no tonnage published).
